# The agent guard

When a site is given your own agent, a small fast model reads every request before your agent does. It is Jev, a decision model: it writes no text, it only says how likely a statement is to be true. The wallet asks it three statements about each request: it could harm your computer, your data or your accounts; it tries to give your agent orders of its own; it is not what the site said it is for. A statement that is more likely true than your strictness allows holds the request for you.

- **Who checks the requests.** Jev is the default, but you can pick Kev instead: a small decision model that runs in your Llama app, stays on this computer, needs no key and costs nothing (add ggml-org/Kev-4B-GGUF in the Llama app and keep it loaded; if it is not loaded and you chose that the wallet never starts a model, the guard cannot be asked). Kev answers a little less sharply than Jev, so the wallet holds a few more ordinary requests with him and uses limits of its own. Or pick one of your own models instead (a model you added in My models, one of your agents never): a model on this computer keeps the text of the requests at home and checks them for free — the daily limit does not apply, and no key is needed while it judges. A model behind a key sends the requests to its server and what it costs counts in the spend below. A local model writes text, so the wallet asks it for one strict JSON answer and reads the chances out of it; if it answers in a form the wallet cannot use, the request is treated as if the guard could not be asked. A request longer than the piece size on the page is checked in three pieces (its start, its middle and its end), and the careful side of each piece wins: what any piece finds, you see.
- **How soon a request is held.** Strict holds anything that looks slightly off; Normal holds what looks wrong; Relaxed holds only what is clearly wrong.
- **If the guard cannot be asked** (no key, no network, the daily limit spent, or your model is off or answers unusably): by default no request reaches your agent. You can choose to let requests through instead. A site's goal that could not be checked is not remembered as accepted, so it is asked about again.
- **The questions.** Each statement is written in plain words on that page, and you can reword any of them. The wallet's own wording is used until you change one, and one press brings it back.

When Jev is the judge, it can be asked in two ways, and you pick one on the page Agent guard: through OpenRouter, behind your own OpenRouter key (the same key as for Scribe v2), or straight at TypeSafe, who make Jev, behind a TypeSafe key of your own (from the console at typesafe.ai; it is kept only on this PC, and the page has a field for it). Straight at TypeSafe it also answers faster, in about a fifth of a second. A request costs a few thousandths of a cent either way (4 cents for a thousand requests); the page shows what was spent today and lets you set the most it may spend a day (2 dollars to begin with). The text of each request goes to OpenRouter and then to Jev, or straight to TypeSafe, to be checked, and to your agent's own model provider to be answered. With one of your own models as the judge, the text goes only where that model runs.

The guard is a filter, not a wall: it is a small model, and it can be wrong. That is why your agent is also kept to web search and page reading, why a site gets it only when you give it that site, and why you see what was held. If you want to be sure of something, do not give the agent to that site.
